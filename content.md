## Screen 2: The claim
Headline: Your ad isn't the problem. The click is where customers get lost.
Body: Ads explain the product to a general buyer. Then the click lands on a page that treats everyone the same. For complex products, that gap between ad and understanding is where people drift.

---

## Screen 3: Why it happens
Shared line: A buyer isn't one person. It's a group: someone who pays, someone who uses, someone who doubts.

Founder:
You earned the click. Then the page hands them a form and a sales call, and understanding becomes your sales team's job.

Marketing Head:
The campaign did its job and earned the click. After it, the story stops: a good narrative in the ad, a feature list on the page.

Product/Growth Head:
Sales keeps answering the same questions: which model, will it work in my home, why this price. The page isn't answering them.

---

## Screen 4: The fix
Headline: Feature lists don't sell complex products. Relatability does.
Body: People don't need everything the product can do. They need to see why it fits their life.
Flow (shown visually):
1. A story in the ad
2. A cliffhanger
3. An interactive page resolves it
4. They understand it for themselves
5. A relevant next step

## Screen 6: Teardown (Eureka Forbes smart robotic vacuum)
Headline: What happens after a good ad
The ad: A strong story. The creative itself never points to the site.
After the click: A "Learn more" button leads to a general product page with three competing actions: buy, book a live demo, or talk to the AI assistant. The assistant asks for a phone number first.
Label (our view): In our view, that's friction for someone still deciding.
Our take: Personalize by home. Humid Mumbai and dry Pune bring different dust. A house with kids and a house with elderly parents spill different things.
Label (hypothesis): We'd test whether prospects who see the product in their own home ask sales fewer basic questions.
Visuals: screenshots from public/teardown
